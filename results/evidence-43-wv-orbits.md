# #43: the reflection subgroup cannot move the root classes — why the three cusped rows are not a search problem

The rows at Sterk's (4.3.7), (4.3.8) and (4.3.9) are the ones still missing from card 029J: two, two and four root
classes of $K_J$ whose $\Gamma_v$-orbits nobody has counted. The last turn established the coarse half of the
question — the whole of $O(K_J)$ identifies every class of a row with every other
(`results/evidence-43-okj-complement.md`) — so the count lies between 1 and the number of classes. This file settles
the fine half, and it is not good news for a computation done here.

## What can be constructed, and what it does

The chamber at the cusp is a fundamental domain of $W_v$, the reflection subgroup of $G_v$, so the reflections in its
walls generate $W_v$ and $W_v \leq \Gamma_v$; every wall is orthogonal to the cusp, so every one of those reflections
fixes the cusp. The walls are the only part of $\Gamma_v$ that the card's data constructs, and the owner's
`root_class_orbits` applies still fewer of them — only the reflections of the classes it is given, that is the
$(-2)$-walls.

`results/probe-43-wv-orbits.py`, host Sage 10.8, Sage only; log `results/evidence-43-wv-orbits.log`:

The search is complete at each depth it reaches — it walks the orbit of one class, so the whole ball is enumerated,
and the ball is a few hundred classes at depth 12 — and the diagram invariant says why no greater depth can help:
in a Coxeter system conjugate standard parabolics are equal, and the setwise stabilizer of a wall's mirror is the
parabolic generated by that wall together with the walls orthogonal to it. Two walls in one $W_v$-orbit therefore
have the same orthogonal neighbour set, and the root walls of every cusped row have pairwise different sets. At
(4.3.8), $\alpha_3$ is orthogonal to $\alpha_9$ and not to $\alpha_{11}$, while $\alpha_{12}$ is orthogonal to
$\alpha_{11}$ and not to $\alpha_9$.

So: the coarsest group that acts puts all the classes in one orbit, and the only part of the stabilizer that this
repository can construct puts every class in an orbit of its own. Neither bound meets, and the gap is exactly the
index of $\Gamma_v$'s image in $O(K_J)$ — that is, the parabolic, non-reflective part of the stabilizer, which a
primitive isotropic line does not supply as a generating family (`generation_obstruction()`,
`sage_extensions/categories/isotropic_orbits.py:3761` in `main`, `:3068` in the image's baked checkout).

## What that means for the rows

The three rows are not waiting on a longer search, a better invariant or a faster machine. They are waiting on one
input this repository cannot produce: **a certified generating family of the cusp stabilizer $\Gamma_v$**, which is
sage-extensions#312's work. A test now keeps the finding: `tests/test_cusp_root_orbits.py`
`test_the_chambers_own_reflections_do_not_move_the_root_classes` computes the invariant from the shipped card and
asserts the root walls' neighbour sets differ pairwise at every cusped row, and the run is 8 passed in 9.29 s.

No card value is written by this probe; the row at (4.3.6) landed in the previous turn
(`lattices/029J.md:491`).
