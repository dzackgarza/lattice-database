This is the even hyperbolic 2-elementary lattice with [invariants](../theory/discriminant-forms.html#two-elementary) $(r, a, \delta) = (10, 10, 0)$.

Since $a = r$, $L^* = \tfrac{1}{2} L$, and $L$ is the twist $M(2)$ of the unimodular lattice $M = U \oplus E_{8}(-1)$, the record [0128](../tag/0128.html).
The corpus records a twist $M(n)$, $|n| \geq 2$, only for the 13 rows $a = r$ of Nikulin's table, because the classification names the twist and not $M$.

Nikulin lists it in Table 1 of *Factor groups of groups of automorphisms of hyperbolic forms with respect to subgroups generated by 2-reflections* (1983), the table of the even hyperbolic lattices $L$ with $2 A_L = 0$ that have a primitive embedding in the K3 lattice.
These are the invariant lattices of the nonsymplectic involutions of K3 surfaces (Remark 4.5.3), and Theorem 4.3.2 states that $(r, a, \delta)$ determines each of them.

$U$ is the lattice with basis $e, f$ and $b(e, e) = b(f, f) = 0$, $b(e, f) = 1$.
$L(k)$ is the module $L$ with the form $k b$.
$E_8$ is the root lattice of that type: $e_1, \dots, e_8$ is a basis of simple roots, numbered as SageMath's `CartanMatrix` numbers them, and $b(e_i, e_j)$ is the entry $(i, j)$ of the Cartan matrix.
The basis of an orthogonal sum is the union of the bases of the summands, in the order written.

The reflection part of $O(L)$ has a fundamental polyhedron with one ideal vertex, and no source prints it: Sterk's (4.3.5), p. 19, prints the twelve-walled chamber of $W_e$ inside $G_e = \{g \in O(M(2)) : \text{the induced map on } M(2)^*/M(2) \text{ respects the decomposition}\}$, a subgroup of $O(M(2))$ that he does not identify with $O(U(2) \oplus E_8(-2))$, and prints its maximal parabolic subdiagrams only *modulo* the symmetries $S_e \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/2$, four of them ((4.3.5), pp. 19–20, Fig. 5).
So the `integral.cusp_chambers` row under $O$ is a computed value and cites no source: `HyperbolicLattices(ZZ)(Lattices(ZZ)(\text{the card's Gram tensor}))` ran Vinberg's algorithm at sage-extensions `5a9874e22ea18fb223ff53cf2a3e48dd63c022b4`, which returned ten walls and ten primitive rays whose squares are $(0, 36, 84, 60, 40, 24, 12, 20, 8, 4)$; exactly one is zero, so the chamber has one ideal vertex and is not cocompact.
That ray is $-f$, of coordinates $(0, -1, 0, \dots, 0)$ in the card's basis, and the walls orthogonal to it are nine, in one component of rank $9 - 1 = 8 = n - 1$ for $n = 9$: a maximal parabolic subdiagram, which is what an ideal vertex is by Vinberg's criterion (È. B. Vinberg, *The groups of units of certain quadratic forms*, Mat. Sb. 87 (1972); Sterk, (4.3.3), p. 18).
$G_e$ itself has no row on this card: `integral.cusp_chambers` keys a non-standard group by the slug of an `arithmetic-groups/` card of this lattice, and no such card defines $G_e$ or $S_e$.

Sterk's group $G_e$ — the image of the stabilizer of $e$ in $O(M(2))$, the subgroup whose induced map on $M(2)^*/M(2)$ respects the decomposition — is the card [G_e](../arithmetic-groups/g-e.html), and its reflection part $W_e$ has a chamber that is not the chamber of $O(L)$: twelve walls, where the lattice's own reflection group has ten.
The chamber is stored in `integral.cusp_chambers['g-e']`, transcribed from Sterk's (4.3.5), p. 19, with Fig. 4: the walls $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_8$, $\alpha_9 = f' - e'$, $\alpha_{10} = \bar\alpha_8 + 2e'$, $\alpha_{11} = 2e' + 2f' + \bar\alpha_1 + \bar\alpha_8$ and $\alpha_{12} = 5e' + 3f' + 2\bar\alpha_2$, all of square $-4$ as the figure circles every node, and their Gram matrix and thirteen-edge Coxeter diagram computed from this card's Gram tensor.
The row states no count of ideal vertices: Fig. 5, p. 20, prints the *maximal parabolic subdiagrams modulo the symmetries* $S_e \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/2$, and Sterk reads that picture as "four equivalence classes ... four labelled classes".
Those four classes are the row's `parabolic_orbits`: the chamber has nine ideal vertices — the primitive rays on the light cone, each a maximal parabolic subdiagram of rank $n - 1 = 8$ by Vinberg's criterion (È. B. Vinberg, *The groups of units of certain quadratic forms*, Mat. Sb. 87 (1972); Sterk, (4.3.3), p. 18) — and the symmetries of the diagram carry them in four orbits, of sizes $1$, $2$, $2$ and $4$.
