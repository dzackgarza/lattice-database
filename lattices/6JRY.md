The lattice $\Lambda_{4} = U \oplus U \oplus E_{8}(-1) \oplus E_{8}(-1) \oplus \langle -4 \rangle$ of signature $(2, 19)$, the primitive cohomology $L_{2}$ of a polarized K3 surface of degree $4$ in Scattone's notation: rank $21$, determinant $-262144$, even.

Its rows state the counts of orbits of primitive isotropic planes, the $1$-cusps of the Baily–Borel compactification — under $O$ 9, which Scattone's Proposition 5.4.7 with Corollary 5.4.8(2), p. 78, and Section 6.3 (Degree four), p. 100, prove, and under $O_-(L_2) = O^+$ 9, the $h(2)$ curves of Section 6, p. 94 — and of primitive isotropic lines, the $0$-cusps: one under $O_-(L_2) = O^+$ (Section 6, p. 94) and one under $\Gamma_2 = O^*(L_2) = \widetilde{O}(L_2) \cap O_-(L_2) = \widetilde{O}^+$ (Theorem 4.0.1, p. 55, whose $\lfloor (N+2)/2 \rfloor$ is one for $N = 1$ in degree $2k = 4$). Those two groups are the card's `O+` and `Otilde+` by Scattone's definitions: $O_-(L)$, the isometries of spinor norm 1 (Section 3.6, p. 50), is the index-two subgroup of $O(L)$ preserving the component $D_k$ of the period domain (Section 1, p. 3), and $O^*(L) = O_-(L) \cap \widetilde{O}(L)$ (Section 3.6, p. 51) is $\Gamma_k$ (Section 1, p. 4).
