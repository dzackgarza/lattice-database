Allcock (Lemma 1) shows that $\mathrm{Aut}(N) \cong \mathrm{Aut}(I_{2,10})$.

Roots: write $r = a e_{1} + c e_{2} + w$ with $w$ in the span $W$ of $e_{3}, \dots, e_{12}$.
$b(e_{1}, e_{2}) = 1$ is odd, $W$ is orthogonal to $e_{1}$ and $e_{2}$, and $b(w, w) \in 4\mathbb{Z}$ for $w$ in $W$, because the Gram tensor of $W$ has its diagonal components in $4\mathbb{Z}$ and the others in $2\mathbb{Z}$.
So $b(r, e_{2}) = a$, $b(r, e_{1}) = c$ and $b(r, r) = 2 a c + b(w, w)$.
If $a$ is odd and $c$ is even, then $b(r, r) \equiv 0 \bmod 4$, and $b(r, r)$ divides $2 b(r, e_{2}) = 2 a \equiv 2 \bmod 4$, which is impossible; the same holds with $a$ and $c$ exchanged.
Each root is therefore in $L' = \{a \equiv c \bmod 2\}$, which has index 2 in $L$.
$e_{1} + e_{2}$, $e_{1} + e_{2} + e_{3}$ and $e_{1} + e_{2} + e_{4}$ have $b(r, r) = 2$ and $b(r, L) = \mathbb{Z}$, and $2$ divides $2$.
$e_{1} - e_{2}$ has $b(r, r) = -2$ and $b(r, L) = \mathbb{Z}$, and $-2$ divides $2$.
$e_{5}$, $e_{6}$, $e_{7}$, $e_{8}$, $e_{9}$, $e_{10}$, $e_{11}$ and $e_{12}$ have $b(r, r) = -4$ and $b(r, L) = 2\mathbb{Z}$, and $-4$ divides $4$.
These roots are in $L'$, and their invariant factors in $L$ are $1, \dots, 1, 2$, so they generate $L'$: $\mathbb{Z}\Phi(L) = L'$, of index 2 in $L$.

## The chambers at the 0-cusps of $\Gamma_{\mathrm{En},2}$ {#cusp-chambers}

The five $0$-cusps of $\Gamma_{\mathrm{En},2}$ are the primitive isotropic vectors of Sterk's Proposition 4.2.3, p. 16, in the basis of $L_- = H \oplus H(2) \oplus E_8(-2)$ above with $e = e_1$, $f = e_2$, $e' = e_3$ and $f' = e_4$: $e$, $e'$, $e' + f' + \omega$ with $\omega$ of square $-4$, $e' + 2f' + \gamma$ with $\gamma$ of square $-8$, and $2e + 2f + \alpha$ with $\alpha$ of square $-8$ (H. Sterk, *Compactifications of the period space of Enriques surfaces, Part I*, Math. Z. 207 (1991), 1–36, (4.2.2)–(4.2.3), p. 16).
In Sterk's notation the $\alpha_i$ are the simple roots of $E_8(-2)$ in the numbering of his Fig. 1, p. 3, whose branch node is $\alpha_4$ — the basis vectors $e_5, \ldots, e_{12}$ above are that tree with the branch at $e_8$, and $\alpha_1 = e_5$, $\alpha_2 = e_6$, $\alpha_3 = e_7$, $\alpha_4 = e_8$, $\alpha_5 = e_9$, $\alpha_6 = e_{10}$, $\alpha_7 = e_{11}$, $\alpha_8 = e_{12}$.
