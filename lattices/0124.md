This is the even hyperbolic 2-elementary lattice with [invariants](../theory/discriminant-forms.html#two-elementary) $(r, a, \delta) = (10, 8, 0)$.

Nikulin lists it in Table 1 of *Factor groups of groups of automorphisms of hyperbolic forms with respect to subgroups generated by 2-reflections* (1983), the table of the even hyperbolic lattices $L$ with $2 A_L = 0$ that have a primitive embedding in the K3 lattice.
These are the invariant lattices of the nonsymplectic involutions of K3 surfaces (Remark 4.5.3), and Theorem 4.3.2 states that $(r, a, \delta)$ determines each of them.

Roots: let $e, f$ be the basis of $U$, and let $r = a e + c f + v$ be a root, with $v$ in $E_8(-2)$.
Write $v = m v'$ with $v'$ primitive, and $m = 0$ when $v = 0$.
$E_8$ is unimodular, so $b(v, E_8(-2)) = 2m\mathbb{Z}$, and $b(r, L) = d\mathbb{Z}$ with $d = \gcd(a, c, 2m)$.
$r$ is primitive, so $\gcd(a, c, m) = 1$, and $d \in \{1, 2\}$.
$b(r, r) = 2ac + b(v, v)$, and 4 divides $b(v, v)$.
$b(r, r)$ divides $2 b(r, x)$ for each $x$ in $L$, so it divides $2d$.
When $d = 2$, $a$ and $c$ are even.
When $d = 1$, $b(r, r)$ divides 2 and is even, so $ac + b(v, v)/2 = \pm 1$, and $a$ and $c$ are odd.
Each root is therefore in $M = \{a \equiv c \bmod 2\}$, which has index 2 in $L$ and the basis $e + f$, $e - f$, $e_1, \dots, e_8$.
Each vector of that basis is a root: $\mathbb{Z}\Phi(L) = M = \mathbb{Z}(e + f) \oplus \mathbb{Z}(e - f) \oplus E_8(-2)$, an orthogonal sum.

The reflection group of $L$ is the whole orthogonal group up to $\pm 1$: Sterk identifies $G_{e'} = O(H \oplus E_8(-2))$ and, as the diagram has no symmetry, concludes $O(H \oplus E_8(-2)) = \{\pm 1\} W_{e'}$ (Math. Z. 207 (1991), (4.3.6), p. 21).
Fig. 6 there prints the Coxeter diagram of a fundamental chamber of it — the nodes $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_8$, $\alpha_9 = \bar\alpha_8 + 2f$ and $\alpha_{10} = e - f$, marked by Sterk's conventions that a $(-4)$-vector is circled and a $(-2)$-vector bulleted and that an arrow points towards the shorter of two adjacent walls — and Fig. 7 prints its two maximal parabolic subdiagrams.
A maximal parabolic subdiagram is a vertex at infinity of the chamber, by Vinberg's criterion that the vertices at infinity of the fundamental polyhedron are the parabolic subdiagrams of rank $n - 1$ ((4.3.3), p. 18, quoting È. B. Vinberg, *The groups of units of certain quadratic forms*, Mat. Sb. 87 (1972)), so the chamber has two ideal vertices, which is the row of `integral.cusp_chambers` under $O$.
The same chamber is the one stored on [029J](../tag/029J.html) at the cusp $\mathbb{Z}e'$ of $\Gamma_{\mathrm{En},2}$, whose quotient $(e')^\perp/\mathbb{Z}e'$ is this lattice ((4.3.4), p. 19).
